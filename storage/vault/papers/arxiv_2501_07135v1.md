# Follow the Leader — Enhancing Systematic Trend-Following Using Network Momentum

## Core Claim

Li & Ferreira (2025) take a classical commodity-futures trend-following stack (univariate MACD-style EMA crossover oscillators at 6 speeds, vol-targeted to σ_tgt = 10%) and **bolt on a cross-asset "network momentum" overlay**. Mechanism in three stages:

1. **Pairwise lead-lag matrix** `V_t ∈ R^{M×M}` (M = 28 commodity futures) per trading day. Two estimator families:
   - **Lévy area** (signature-based; detects both linear and nonlinear lead-lag at fixed lag ℓ = ±1 day) — Theorem 1 of Reference [26].
   - **Dynamic Time Warping** family — DTW / DDTW (derivative DTW) / shapeDTW / shapeDDTW (multidimensional descriptors of length l = 11). Lag = `Mode(j − i)` over the optimal warping path.
2. **Graph learning** (Kalofolias 2016; convex optimization solved with CVXPY+MOSEK): map the dense skew-symmetric `V_t` into a **sparse non-negative adjacency matrix** `A_t` via `min_A tr(V^T (D−A) V) − α 1^T log(A1) + β ‖A‖_F^2`. Optionally ensemble across lookback windows δ ∈ {22, 44, 66, 88, 110, 132} days then average and graph-normalize `Ã = D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2}`.
3. **Network momentum feature** = neighbor-weighted sum of own-style oscillators: `R̃^k_{t,m} = Σ_{n∈N(m)} Ã_{m,n} R^k_{t,n}` for each speed k. Plug into the same volatility-scaled position formula as MACD with sigmoid response `r(x) = c_λ x e^{−λ²x²/2}` (λ = √2).

Headline empirics on 100 stationary-block-bootstrapped trajectories (in-sample 2002→2005, out-of-sample 2005→Jun 2024, 28 commodity-futures contracts):

| Model | Net Sharpe | Sharpe Δ vs MACD | Net Return | Skewness | MDD | Sign-agreement w/ MACD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MACD baseline | 0.277 | — | 0.030 | 0.395 | 0.239 | — |
| NMM-DTW-E | 0.353 | +27% | 0.039 | 0.457 | 0.259 | 87% |
| NMM-DDTW-E | **0.357** | **+29%** | 0.039 | 0.486 | 0.250 | 86% |
| NMM-LEVY | 0.336 | +21% | 0.036 | 0.419 | **0.230** | 89% |

Wilcoxon signed-rank: every NMM variant beats MACD with p < 0.05 (most p ≈ 0). PnL-correlation with MACD ranges 0.71–0.89 — i.e. **the network signal is mostly recapitulating the trend signal**, with a thin slice (~13–15% of days where signs disagree) producing the alpha lift. On real (non-bootstrap) data the lift is similar but noisier: NMM-DTW-E real Sharpe = 0.364 vs MACD 0.233 (+56%).

The contribution that is genuinely interesting (not just plumbing) is **§6.4 skewness-by-horizon**: NMM models keep more positive skew at the 2-month return horizon than MACD, hinting that the network overlay is improving **medium-term timing** rather than daily directional accuracy.

## Aha Moment

The transferable kernel is not the graph-learning machinery — it is the meta-claim that **a slow lead-lag relation between assets exists at the daily horizon and is detectable by sign-asymmetric path statistics (Lévy area), not just by Pearson covariance** — i.e. a temporally directed cross-asset signal that symmetric Cov(·,·,N) provably cannot represent.

## Candidate Ideas

### Idea 1 — Cross-stock pairwise lead-lag matrix → graph-learned network momentum (paper's full mechanism, csi1000 port)

- **Paper mechanism**: build `V_t ∈ R^{1000×1000}` of pairwise DTW or Lévy-area lead-lag scores on csi1000 daily returns over δ = 132d, learn sparse adjacency by CVXPY convex optimization, weight neighbor momenta into network signal.
- **Target frequency**: daily
- **Current readiness**: `blocked_by_architecture`
- **Required fields**: `$close` (returns) — fields are fine; the **machinery** is the block.
- **Why it may survive daily downsampling**: irrelevant — paper itself is daily.
- **Main distortion risk**: M = 1000 nodes vs paper's M = 28 means **N² = 1M pairs per rebuild day**, and graph-learning convex solve is `O(N³)` worst-case. Even setting compute aside, statistical estimation of a 1M-edge sparse adjacency from 132 daily samples is **rank-deficient by 4 orders of magnitude** — the curse of dimensionality is qualitatively different from 28-asset commodities.
- **Suggested direction tag**: null (blocked)

### Idea 2 — Industry-relative lead-lag (degraded: stock vs sector-mean lagged return)

- **Paper mechanism**: paper §1 cites Hou (2007) finding industry affiliation drives lead-lag — large-cap industry leaders predict small-cap industry followers next-day. Approximate by: own forward return predicted by **industry-peer-mean lagged return**.
- **Target frequency**: daily
- **Current readiness**: `blocked_by_data`
- **Required fields**: would need an **industry-membership table** (e.g. CITIC L1/L2 sector code per stock per date) joined into the cross-section before any DSL `Mean` can compute peer-mean. The DSL whitelist has no industry field; `CsRank` / `CsZscore` are universe-wide, not sector-grouped.
- **Why it may survive daily downsampling**: lead-lag is by construction a daily-or-coarser phenomenon in equities; sub-daily lead-lag belongs in HFT.
- **Main distortion risk**: even if the table is loaded, csi1000 has 1000 stocks across ~30 CITIC L1 sectors → mean ~33 stocks/sector → peer-mean is a very stable construct, but **the construct collapses to "lagged sector return"** which is then highly correlated with overall market beta-lag — which is itself a risk-factor exposure, not idiosyncratic alpha. Will hit Barra style absorption in CP02.
- **Suggested direction tag**: null (blocked, requires data + likely Barra-absorbed)

### Idea 3 — Universe-mean lagged return → own forward (degraded further: no industry table)

- **Paper mechanism**: same spillover idea but using csi1000-wide cross-sectional aggregate as the "leader" — `CsMean(ret_{t-1})` predicts each stock's own `ret_t`.
- **Target frequency**: daily
- **Current readiness**: `dsl_ready` but **already disproven by adjacency**.
- **Required fields**: `$close` only.
- **Why it may survive daily downsampling**: it does not survive — csi1000 cross-sectional mean lagged return is **the market factor at lag 1**, which is exactly the universe-wide return autocorrelation, well-known to be ~0 at the 1-day horizon for A-share post-2017.
- **Main distortion risk**: this candidate is structurally identical to the rate / Δ-form return signals already mass-rejected in `return_momentum_acceleration` (dead, batch_029) and `asymmetric_momentum` (dead, batch_028). The "leader" reduces to lagged xs return, which is rate-form geometry — **already a system-level lesson F300 in `lessons.md`**.
- **Suggested direction tag**: null (do not test — would replay a closed lesson)

### Idea 4 — Own-stock Lévy-area between return and signed-volume change (single-stock cross-modal lead)

- **Paper mechanism**: rewrite Lévy area equation (1) on a 2-D path `(ret_t, Δvol_t)` of one stock over a window, instead of `(ret_t^i, ret_t^j)` of two stocks. The Lévy area is **antisymmetric in the two coordinates**, so `A^Lévy(ret, Δvol) = (1/N) Σ [ret_s · Δvol_{s−1} − Δvol_s · ret_{s−1}]` measures whether **return leads volume change** or vice versa over the window. Symmetric Pearson `Cov(ret, Δvol, N)` cannot represent this temporal directionality — it is mathematically orthogonal to F009 / F001 which are symmetric in time.
- **Target frequency**: daily
- **Current readiness**: `dsl_ready` (Sum, Sub, Mul, Ref are whitelisted)
- **Required fields**: `$close`, `$volume` (or `$amount`)
- **Why it may survive daily downsampling**: the antisymmetric kernel is constructed at daily resolution by definition.
- **Main distortion risk**: **`pv_covariance` is dead** (batch_039) with explicit lesson "volume × direction composite → reversal cluster around F001/F009/F012". The Lévy-area construction is mathematically independent of symmetric covariance, but **empirically** in csi1000 it will likely still load on the same reversal factor at the cross-section after rank-normalization, because both estimators are weighted sums of the same `ret × vol` cross-products. The chance this escapes the F001/F009/F012 attractor is low — maybe 1-in-5 odds the antisymmetric component carries genuinely new info — and it does not justify a new direction; if revisited it should be a single candidate inside `pv_covariance` or `intraday_price_formation` after the attractor analysis is re-examined.
- **Suggested direction tag**: null (one candidate's worth of value, not a direction)

### Idea 5 — Sigmoid response curve `r(x) = c·x·exp(−λ²x²/2)` as cross-section preprocessor

- **Paper mechanism**: §5 squashes raw oscillator signals through a reverting sigmoid before forming positions, attenuating extreme tails so risk doesn't blow up at λ = √2 (response peaks at ±1/λ).
- **Target frequency**: daily
- **Current readiness**: `python_ready` but mis-located — this is a **portfolio-construction transform**, not a factor.
- **Required fields**: any factor output.
- **Why it may survive daily downsampling**: it's an output transform, not a signal.
- **Main distortion risk**: our system evaluates IC, monotonicity, long-short t-stat on **rank-normalized** cross-sections (CP01-CP06). Sigmoid squashing of a rank-normalized factor is **rank-preserving** within the sigmoid's monotone region — therefore IC, Spearman-mono, ls_t are invariant. This transform belongs in **portfolio-construction layer (post-admit)**, not in factor mining. Out-of-scope for `/factor-paper`.
- **Suggested direction tag**: null (architectural mis-fit — not a factor)

## Data Requirements

**Paper dependencies**:
- Daily settlement prices for 28 commodity futures (Bloomberg tickers, backward Panama back-adjusted) — N ≈ 28 nodes is the entire empirical universe.
- Bid-ask half-spread per market (used only for transaction-cost accounting; we don't model TC at admission gate so this is moot).
- 22-day EWM volatility estimator (we have `Std`, `EMA` — fine).
- A convex graph-learning solver (CVXPY + MOSEK) — not in our stack.

**What we lack for a faithful port**:
- **Convex optimization layer** — would require Python escape hatch with `cvxpy` import (not in the import whitelist `numpy pandas scipy math typing dataclasses functools itertools __future__`).
- **DTW algorithm** (and DDTW / shapeDTW variants) — not a DSL operator and not in the Python escape-hatch import whitelist.
- **Lévy-area pairwise estimator at universe scale** — could be coded with pure NumPy (it's just summation of `r_s · r_{s-1}^T − r_{s-1} · r_s^T`), but with M = 1000 stocks the per-day matrix is 1M entries × 132 lookback samples — feasible compute but **statistically unsound** (1M parameters from 132 obs/day).
- **Industry-membership table** for the degraded "industry-relative" port — not in the field whitelist; would need new loader work.
- **Larger-cap → smaller-cap leader assignment** — would need market-cap percentile join into cross-section pre-aggregation, which DSL also cannot express.

**DSL operator gap summary**:

| Paper construct | Equivalent in our DSL | Status |
|---|---|---|
| EMA (paper §4.2 oscillator) | `EMA(x, span)` | ✓ |
| Vol-scaled price delta `Δ̃ = Δ / σ22` | `Div(Delta($close,1), Std(Delta($close,1), 22))` | ✓ |
| EMA-fast minus EMA-slow MACD oscillator | `Sub(EMA(P̃, 2k), EMA(P̃, M·2k))` | ✓ but **rate-form attractor** (F300 lesson) |
| Pairwise Lévy area `Σ (r_s · r_{s-1}^T − r_{s-1} · r_s^T)` | constructible per-stock-pair via `Sub(Mul(x, Ref(y,1)), Mul(y, Ref(x,1)))`, summed | **infeasible at N² = 1M pairs** |
| DTW / DDTW / shapeDTW | none | **blocked_by_architecture** |
| Convex graph learning (CVXPY) | none | **blocked_by_architecture** |
| Cross-asset neighbor aggregation `Σ_{n∈N(m)} A_{m,n} R^k_{t,n}` | requires per-stock peer-set; CsRank/CsZscore are universe-wide | **blocked_by_architecture** |

## Mapping To Current System

The paper's machinery has three layers; we can map each layer's status candidly:

1. **Layer 1 — univariate oscillator (MACD-style EMA crossover at 6 speeds)**: structurally **identical to candidates already proven dead**. `Sub(EMA_fast, EMA_slow)` is a rate / spread form on smoothed return — `return_momentum_acceleration` C001 (`Sub(Mean(ret,5), Mean(ret,20))`) was rejected at ls_t = −0.81 + monotonicity = −0.40, and that result was generalized into `lessons.md` F300 as a system-level rate-form failure law. Replicating the MACD oscillator is **forbidden replay** of a closed lesson.

2. **Layer 2 — pairwise lead-lag detection (Lévy area / DTW)**: **architecturally blocked**. DTW family has no operator. Lévy area pairwise is computationally and statistically infeasible at N = 1000 nodes from 132d windows. Single-stock Lévy area on `(ret, Δvol)` is the only DSL-expressible degraded variant, and it lands in the dead `pv_covariance` attractor with high probability.

3. **Layer 3 — convex graph learning into sparse adjacency**: **architecturally blocked**. No convex solver in DSL or Python escape hatch. Even if added, sparse-adjacency estimation from 132 obs / 1000² parameters is statistically unsound.

The cleanest "honest local port" — universe-mean lagged return predicting own forward return — is **already a closed lesson via** `return_momentum_acceleration` and `asymmetric_momentum`.

The only single candidate worth preserving as a future revisit needle is **single-stock Lévy area on `(ret, Δvol)`** — and even that is more likely to collapse into the F001/F009/F012 reversal cluster than to escape it.

**Conclusion**: no new direction is justified.

## What The Paper Is Hiding

1. **Commodity-futures world ≠ csi1000 A-share world** (most load-bearing hidden assumption). The paper builds **the entire empirical case on 28 commodity-futures contracts** with no shorting friction, T+0 settlement, no listing-event risk, no daily price-limit boundary, and no behavioural retail-dominated flow. csi1000 is 1000 retail-dominated A-share names with ±10% daily limits, T+1 settlement, prevalent ST regime risk, and short-side largely impossible. The paper's lead-lag mechanism (slow sector-spillover at 1d lag among 28 deeply-liquid global futures) **does not generalize structurally** to 1000 retail-dominated daily-limited equities. The paper shows generalization across sectors *within commodities* — it shows nothing about generalization to equities, much less Chinese small-cap equities.

2. **N = 28 → N = 1000 is a curse-of-dimensionality phase transition, not a smooth scaling**. Pairwise lead-lag has 28² = 784 entries to estimate from each 132-day window — that's ~6 obs per parameter, marginal but workable. At csi1000 it's 1M parameters from 132 obs — **6 obs per 1000 parameters**, which is statistically meaningless. The paper neither encounters nor discusses this regime. Sparsity penalties cannot rescue it: the convex graph-learning sparsity floor is `O(M log M)` non-zeros, ≈ 7000 edges for csi1000 — still vastly under-determined.

3. **Transaction-cost accounting is real but the costs are still tiny by design**. Paper §5 uses half-bid-ask spread and reports net-of-cost results. NMM-DTW-E reports gross 0.063, transaction 0.023, net 0.039 → **transaction cost = 37% of gross**. For futures this is plausible. For csi1000 daily-rebalancing with stamp duty + commission + impact, real transaction cost easily reaches 40–80 bps round-trip per portfolio churn day. The paper's net Sharpe lift of +29% over baseline could **fully evaporate** at A-share cost levels. There is no robustness check at higher cost regimes in the paper.

4. **PnL correlation 0.71–0.89 with the MACD baseline means most of the network signal is just trend**. Sign-agreement is 85–90%. So the network overlay is producing alpha **only on the 10–15% of days when it disagrees with MACD** — Table 6 shows average opposing-day PnL gain of 0.011–0.057 annualized, a thin slice of the +29% Sharpe story. This is fragile inference: noise in 10% of trading days dominates the apparent improvement, and the Wilcoxon p-values come from the bootstrap-paired matching, not from genuine independent observations.

5. **132-day lookback is not robustness-tested**. δ ∈ {22, 44, 66, 88, 110, 132} is used in the **ensemble** to *reduce variance*, not to test sensitivity to the choice itself. A single δ = 132 (paper's primary configuration) is one knob. There's no out-of-sample validation that δ = 132 is itself the right choice — the lookback was likely tuned implicitly during model design on the same 2002–2024 horizon.

6. **Backtest period overlaps with training period**. Paper §4.1 says training spans **June 2002 to June 2024** but out-of-sample evaluation is **January 2005 to June 2024** — i.e. the OOS window is *inside* the training window. Hyperparameter grid-search for α, β was done on "in-sample data" without the cut-point being explicitly defined. This is a significant evaluation hygiene issue not flagged in the paper.

## Blocked Ideas For Future

These are not dead — they are unblocked when a specific architecture or data piece arrives. Listed by **minimum unblock condition**:

- **Industry-relative lead-lag spillover** (Idea 2). **Unblock condition**: (a) load CITIC L1/L2 industry-membership table into DSL whitelist as `$citic_l1` (constant per stock per period), (b) add `CsGroupMean(x, group)` operator. With both pieces, the family `Mean of group-peer lagged return` becomes a clean test of Hou (2007)-style industry spillover at A-share daily horizon. Even then, expect Barra industry-factor absorption to dominate.

- **Cross-stock single-pair Lévy-area lead-lag detector** (Idea 1, partial). **Unblock condition**: introduce a cross-stock pair operator `CsPairwise(x, y, kernel)` returning a per-stock score = aggregate of Lévy-area scores against top-K nearest peers (e.g. by market-cap or sector). This avoids the 1M-edge estimation problem by restricting to top-K neighborhoods. Still requires a "neighbor" definition that the system currently lacks.

- **Own-stock Lévy area on `(ret, Δvol)` as a single revisit candidate inside `pv_covariance`** (Idea 4). **Unblock condition**: re-opening `pv_covariance` direction (currently dead). One candidate, expressed as `Sum(Sub(Mul(x, Ref(y,1)), Mul(Ref(x,1), y)), 20)` with x = daily return, y = log-volume change. **Pre-condition**: re-examine the F001/F009/F012 reversal-cluster attractor analysis to determine whether antisymmetric kernels are tested in the original cluster basis. If they are not, this candidate is one CP01-CP06 admission attempt (≤1 mt-budget cost).

- **Sigmoid response transform in portfolio construction layer** (Idea 5). **Unblock condition**: portfolio-construction module exists (it does not — current system stops at admitted-factor reports + library composition by Phase 5). Out-of-scope until the project gets a portfolio layer.

## Direction Recommendation

- **Decision**: `do_not_create_direction`
- **Reason**:
  - The paper's headline mechanism (graph-learned cross-asset network momentum from pairwise DTW / Lévy-area estimators) is **`blocked_by_architecture`**: no DTW operator, no convex graph-learning solver, and the N = 28 → N = 1000 scaling is a statistical phase transition the paper does not address.
  - The two DSL-expressible degradations both replay closed lessons:
    - Universe-mean lagged return → own forward = exactly the rate / Δ-form geometry **already proven dead** in `return_momentum_acceleration` (batch_029) and codified as system-level lesson F300.
    - Single-stock Lévy area on `(ret, Δvol)` is mathematically antisymmetric and thus formally orthogonal to `Cov(ret, vol)`, but the broader `volume × direction` composite family has been mass-reverted via `pv_covariance` (batch_039 dead) plus the F004 升格 lesson "volume × direction composite → reversal cluster". This is at most a single revisit candidate inside the dead `pv_covariance` direction, not a new direction.
  - The MACD oscillator baseline itself is `Sub(EMA_fast, EMA_slow)` — already covered by F300 rate-form failure law.
- **Minimum unblock conditions** (any one re-opens parts of this paper):
  - **For Idea 1 / 2 (any cross-stock spillover)**: introduce industry-membership field into DSL whitelist + `CsGroupMean(x, group)` operator. Then the Hou (2007)-style industry leader → industry follower lagged-return test becomes a clean candidate family.
  - **For Idea 1 (faithful)**: add Python-escape-hatch import for a DTW library and a convex graph-learning solver (cvxpy or scipy.optimize-based bespoke implementation). Even then, pre-flight a statistical-feasibility check: 1M-parameter sparse-adjacency estimation from 132d windows is not viable at N = 1000.
  - **For Idea 4**: re-open `pv_covariance` for one antisymmetric-kernel revisit candidate, gated on a re-examination of whether the F001/F009/F012 attractor analysis tested antisymmetric (vs symmetric) kernels.
- **Reviewer's overall judgment**: this is a well-executed commodity-futures paper that does not transfer to A-share daily csi1000 in any non-trivial way. The 27–29% Sharpe lift is meaningful in a 28-asset deeply-liquid futures world; the same machinery applied to 1000 retail-dominated daily-limited equities would face simultaneous statistical infeasibility (N²/T ratio), economic infeasibility (transaction cost), and structural-overlap infeasibility (oscillator family ≡ dead rate-form lesson). No direction is created.
