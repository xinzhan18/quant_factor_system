# Discovery of a 13-Sharpe OOS Factor — Drift Regimes Unlock Hidden Cross-Sectional Predictability (Singha 2025)

## Core Claim

A single-author NASA-affiliation note claiming a `Sharpe = 13.19` long-short factor on S&P 500 (2010-11 / 2015-16 / 2020-21 walk-forward), built from **two off-the-shelf weak signals** wrapped in a **stock-specific drift regime gate**:

- `value_i,t = CsRank(1 / $close_i,t)` (inverse price, percentile-ranked)
- `reversal_i,t = -CsZscore(Mean(daily_return_i, 10))` (10d cumulative return, sign-flipped, z-scored)
- `BASE_i,t = 0.7 * value + 0.3 * reversal`
- `UpFraction_i,t = (1/63) * Σ_{k=1..63} I[ret_i,t-k > 0]`
- `REGIME_i,t = I[UpFraction_i,t > 0.60]`
- `EDGE_i,t = BASE * REGIME` — multiplicative gate that zeroes out the BASE signal whenever the stock is NOT in drift regime
- Portfolio: dollar-neutral L/S z-score, vol/MDD-scaled, market-on-close execution, kill-switch overlays

The interaction term contributes 53% of return decomposition (Sharpe 7.2 of 13.2). Without the regime gate the BASE signal Sharpe collapses to **1.2** — i.e., the whole edge is the gate.

Universe: **current** S&P 500 constituents (admitted survivorship bias by author, claimed 20-30% inflation impact). Three 1-year OOS windows totalling 3 years. Author concedes "performance moderates with real-world implementation" but defends the core insight.

---

## Aha Moment

**The signal-quality problem reframed as a sample-selection problem**: instead of building a stronger factor, gate an admittedly weak factor down to the subset of stock-days where the signal carries information. UpFraction>60% is a **stock-by-stock state mask** (not a market-wide regime), so on any given day different stocks are in/out of regime and the strategy stays diversified. In our daily csi1000 context this matters because we have dozens of admitted weak/borderline factors (Grade C / D, alpha_survival in 0.4-0.7) that get rejected one decimal place short of admission — most of them might survive if multiplied by a per-stock activation gate that selectively switches them off in non-trending regimes. The gate is a one-line DSL expression: `Gt(Mean(Gt(Sub($close, Ref($close,1)), 0), 63), 0.6)`.

## Candidate Ideas

### Idea 1 — UpFraction regime gate × CsRank(1/$close) (value channel exact replica)

- **Paper mechanism**: gate inverse-price percentile rank by `Mean(I[ret>0], 63) > 0.6`; long high-rank, short low-rank, but only within regime-active stocks
- **Target frequency**: daily
- **Current readiness**: dsl_ready
- **Required fields**: `$close`, `Ref($close,1)`
- **Why it may survive daily downsampling**: paper itself is daily — no downsampling required. Inverse price as value proxy is the most A-share-honest part (no need for accounting data, robust to reporting-period NaN traps that killed b068-b072 fundamental factors)
- **Main distortion risk**: A-share inverse-price is a known size proxy on csi1000 (low-priced stocks are predominantly small-caps after stock splits don't exist). `|corr|>0.3 to $market_cap` red line in lessons.md will likely trip — must orthogonalize inverse-price against $circ_market_cap or just use it as a baseline that is expected to fail the size-proxy gate
- **Suggested direction tag**: `up_fraction_regime_gating`

### Idea 2 — UpFraction regime gate × short-term reversal (5d / 10d negated mean return)

- **Paper mechanism**: gate `-Mean(ret, 10)` by drift regime; reversal becomes profitable only when the stock has been trending (i.e., during drift, the trend creates short-term overshoot that reverses)
- **Target frequency**: daily
- **Current readiness**: dsl_ready
- **Required fields**: `$close`, `Ref($close,1)`, `Ref($close,k)` for return windows
- **Why it may survive daily downsampling**: short-term reversal on csi1000 is **already known to work** — our system has multiple admitted reversal factors (F011 williams_r_variant, F025 vol_confirmed_reversal_5, F044 price_reversal_60). The novelty is whether the UpFraction gate **strengthens** an existing reversal factor by pruning non-drifting stocks where reversal noise dominates
- **Main distortion risk**: csi1000 reversal in 2022-2023 regime drift period (from lessons.md round 73) was the killing field for `return_momentum_acceleration` and `asymmetric_momentum` — both DEAD. The gate may simply re-activate during late-2020 small-cap mania and then turn off during 2022-2023, producing time-clustered alpha (path-dependence problem the paper itself flags)
- **Suggested direction tag**: `up_fraction_regime_gating`

### Idea 3 — UpFraction regime gate × admitted weak factor (meta-pattern revival path)

- **Paper mechanism**: extend the gate concept to ANY existing borderline factor — gate F022 range_compression_60 (Grade C, score 62.6) or F018 alpha017 (Grade D, score 34.4) by UpFraction>0.6. Hypothesis: borderline factors often have an OOS-stable "regime sub-population" that gets averaged out across all stock-days
- **Target frequency**: daily
- **Current readiness**: dsl_ready (gate is a multiplicative mask over an existing DSL expression)
- **Required fields**: depends on chosen base factor + `$close`, `Ref($close,1)` for the gate
- **Why it may survive daily downsampling**: this is the **only candidate family that doesn't already have admitted twins in the library**. Our 51 factors are all unconditional; gated versions live in unexplored geometry. Rejected/reserve candidates from `reserve_revival_paths` (the existing direction for retro audits of reserves) might be perfect inputs
- **Main distortion risk**: max_lib_corr will be dominated by the un-gated parent factor. Gate × parent corr will be high if regime mask is true >35% of the time (paper's avg). May trip CP05 redundancy gate. Defensive design: pick base factors with current corr-to-library < 0.5 so even partially-conditional version stays under 0.7
- **Suggested direction tag**: `up_fraction_regime_gating`

### Idea 4 — UpFraction value as a continuous standalone signal (NOT a binary gate)

- **Paper mechanism**: paper uses I[UpFraction > 0.6] as a hard binary mask. Alternative: use UpFraction itself (continuous, [0,1]) as a cross-sectional signal — high UpFraction stocks may be persistent trenders whose forward return distribution is asymmetric
- **Target frequency**: daily
- **Current readiness**: dsl_ready
- **Required fields**: `$close`, `Ref($close,1)`
- **Why it may survive daily downsampling**: pure daily count signal with no normalization controversy
- **Main distortion risk**: this is a thin disguise for "win-rate in a window" which structurally collapses to recent return cumulative direction. Likely high corr to existing momentum geometry that already dead in `return_momentum_acceleration` / `asymmetric_momentum`. **High prior probability of redundancy with some F-numbered factor's cumulative-direction encoding**
- **Suggested direction tag**: `up_fraction_regime_gating` (control candidate to validate that the gate ≠ the base signal)

### Idea 5 — Inverted regime gate (low-UpFraction subset)

- **Paper mechanism**: paper claims `BASE * REGIME` works. By construction, `BASE * (1 - REGIME)` should NOT work (or should reverse sign) — a falsification test. Useful as a control, not as a primary admission candidate
- **Target frequency**: daily
- **Current readiness**: dsl_ready
- **Required fields**: `$close`, `Ref($close,1)`
- **Why it may survive daily downsampling**: not designed to survive — designed to refute the binary-state claim
- **Main distortion risk**: if BOTH gates work, the gate is doing nothing and the signal is just BASE. If neither works, BASE itself is dead in csi1000 (likely). If only the inverted gate works, paper's mechanism is structurally inverted in A-share (very plausible given known csi1000 momentum-reversal sign asymmetry)
- **Suggested direction tag**: `up_fraction_regime_gating` (placed in batch_001 as ablation)

## Data Requirements

**Paper dependencies**:
- Daily OHLC for S&P 500 — we have csi1000 equivalent
- 63d trailing window of daily returns — trivially available via `Ref($close, k)` chain
- 10d trailing returns — trivially available
- Inverse price — `1/$close` or `Power($close, -1)`; we also have `$pb_ratio` as an A-share-native value proxy
- Cross-sectional rank (`CsRank`) and z-score (`CsZscore`) — available
- L/S portfolio with vol scaling — out of scope for our factor system (we evaluate cross-sectional ranking IC, not portfolio simulation)

**What we don't need but the paper has**:
- Market-on-close execution mechanics — irrelevant for IC-based admission
- Vol/MDD scaling factor `s* = min(12%/TrainVol, 15%/|TrainMaxDD|)` — irrelevant
- Kill-switch overlays — irrelevant
- $100M-$500M capacity analysis using square-root impact model — irrelevant for csi1000 IC mining

**DSL operator mapping**:
- `Mean(Gt(Sub($close, Ref($close,1)), 0), 63)` → UpFraction (Mean of Boolean → fraction)
- `Gt(UpFraction, 0.6)` → REGIME mask
- `Mul(BASE, REGIME)` → EDGE
- `CsRank(Power($close, -1))` → value component (or `Div(1, $close)`)
- `Mul(Mean(Sub($close, Ref($close,1)), 10), -1)` → reversal component (Neg forbidden, multiply by -1)

All operators in whitelist. No Python escape hatch needed.

## Mapping To Current System

**Where the paper directly overlaps with us**:

- **Reversal**: Library has F011 / F025 / F044 admitted reversal factors. Pure-reversal `-Mean(ret, 10)` candidate likely max_lib_corr > 0.7 to F025/F044
- **Value**: Library has F028 inverse_pb (Grade C) — a similar structure to inverse-price. `1/$close` may also be a stealth size proxy and trip the lessons.md size-proxy red line
- **Composite value × reversal**: `value_liquidity_interaction` direction exists; QA paper (arxiv_2602_07085v2) Channel 1 already produced gap_acceptance factors. Pure value + reversal LINEAR combination is **not** what's novel here — the **gate** is

**Where the paper doesn't overlap**:

- **UpFraction-gated signals** as a class — no admitted factor uses a binary multiplicative mask conditioned on a 63d window of return-sign counts. Searched 32 directions, zero use this geometry
- **Stock-specific (not market-wide) regime conditioning** — `trend_quality_gated` (DEAD) was conceptually adjacent but used continuous gates (turnover-weighted, Sharpe-like) NOT a binary 0/1 mask, and gated momentum (which is itself dead on csi1000) rather than reversal/value (which lives)

**Adjacent dead direction lesson — important**:

- `return_momentum_acceleration` (DEAD batch_029) — proved csi1000 cross-section has no rate-of-change momentum alpha
- `asymmetric_momentum` (DEAD batch_028) — proved sign-conditional return decomposition fails
- `trend_quality_gated` (DEAD batch_037) — proved gating MOMENTUM doesn't work on csi1000 (signal flips from continuation to reversal)

**Critical distinction**: paper's BASE is `value + REVERSAL`, not momentum. The dead directions all gated/decomposed momentum. Reversal is the OPPOSITE of momentum on csi1000 and is known to live. So the `trend_quality_gated` failure does NOT pre-disprove this paper — but it is a serious warning about the regime mechanism transferring at all.

**Path**: DSL only. No Python required. Gate composition is one extra Mul over an existing expression.

## What The Paper Is Hiding

1. **S&P 500 current-constituent survivorship is decisive, not "20-30% inflation"** — author admits the bias but waves it away. The S&P 500 constituent list as of 2024 includes only firms that survived through 2024; gating value+reversal on UpFraction on these survivors during 2010-2011 / 2015-2016 / 2020-2021 means every "drift regime" in the sample retrospectively belongs to a stock that ultimately did NOT delist. UpFraction>60% on a stock that goes bankrupt in 2018 produces a different forward return distribution than UpFraction>60% on Apple in 2010. csi1000 also rebalances annually but our universe definition snapshots survivors per backtest window, so this is mitigated — **but** the paper's headline 13.2 Sharpe is structurally unattainable in our setup. Realistic expectation: 1-3x our existing F009/F043 Grade A factors, not 13x

2. **UpFraction>0.6 with kill-switches and dynamic position scaling = three layers of regime conditioning, not one** — the abstract sells "binary regime gate" as the innovation, but Section 2.2 layers in (a) volatility-target scaling `s* = min(12%/vol, 15%/|MDD|)`, (b) -30% drawdown kill-switch, (c) -10% rolling 63d kill-switch, (d) 3x volatility-spike kill-switch. Table 10 reports zero kill-switch triggers in OOS — but the parameters were FIT on training data including the 2008 crisis, so the 12%/15% scaling is implicitly conditioned on having seen 2008. This is **multiple-testing-ish at the parameter-selection level** that the 1000-randomization test doesn't catch (randomizing the regime mask doesn't randomize the scaling parameters). For us this is a dampening warning — the IC we measure is NOT a Sharpe and won't include any of these portfolio-construction layers, so the paper's robustness argument doesn't transfer

3. **63d window aligned with quarter boundaries — possible reporting-period leakage** — 63 trading days ≈ 1 quarter. UpFraction on the trailing 63 days of returns is a quarter-aligned regime indicator; for S&P 500 stocks, quarterly earnings days are deterministic and clustered. The 60% threshold is suspicious: 0.60 × 63 ≈ 38 up-days, leaving ~25 down-days, almost the exact distribution of "post-earnings drift" stocks. The author does not test parameter robustness around 63 (they only do ±30% on the threshold 0.60); 63 itself is not stress-tested against, say, 50d or 80d. **Hidden assumption**: UpFraction>0.6 over exactly 63d is not just a regime indicator, it may be a quarterly post-earnings-drift selector by accident

4. **The "interaction term contributes 53%" decomposition is internally inconsistent** — Table 8 reports "Value alone in regime: 27%, Reversal alone in regime: 20%, Interaction: 53%". But the paper's BASE is a LINEAR combination `0.7*value + 0.3*reversal`. There is no nonlinear interaction term in the formula. The "interaction" line must be capturing something else — possibly the gate itself (REGIME's contribution beyond what value+reversal alone produce). If so, the gate alone is the alpha and the value+reversal could be replaced by ANY weak signal — which would mean Idea 3 (gated borderline factors) is the truly transferable idea, not Ideas 1/2

5. **Single-author preprint, NASA Goddard astrophysics affiliation, no peer review, JEL codes manually added** — author email is from NASA / Catholic University Physics department (mainak.singha@nasa.gov, singham@cua.edu). No track record in finance journals, no co-authors, no citation by any subsequent finance paper as of 2026-05. The paper is on arXiv q-fin.RT (only 4 days old as of writing). Sharpe 13 walk-forward results that beat every published large-cap factor by an order of magnitude have a long history of disappearing under independent replication. **Default attitude**: treat the regime-gate insight as a worthwhile structural experiment, NOT as evidence that the strategy works at scale

## Blocked Ideas For Future

- **Portfolio simulation with vol/MDD scaling and kill-switches**: our Phase 2/3 evaluates IC and rank stability, not Sharpe with portfolio construction. **Unblock condition**: portfolio simulator with paper-style risk overlays added to research module
