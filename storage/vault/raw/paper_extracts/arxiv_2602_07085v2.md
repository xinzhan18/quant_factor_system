## Title

**QuantaAlpha: An Evolutionary Framework for LLM-Driven Alpha Mining**

(Han, Zhang, Li, Yang, et al. — SUFE / QuantaAlpha / Stanford / PKU / SYSU / SEU, arXiv 2602.07085v2)

## Abstract

Financial markets are noisy and non-stationary, making alpha mining highly sensitive to noise in backtesting results and sudden market regime shifts. While recent agentic frameworks improve alpha mining automation, they often lack controllable multi-round search and reliable reuse of validated experience. To address these challenges, we propose **QuantaAlpha**, an evolutionary alpha mining framework that treats each end-to-end mining run as a trajectory and improves factors through trajectory-level mutation and crossover operations. QuantaAlpha localizes suboptimal steps in each trajectory for targeted revision and recombines complementary high-reward segments to reuse effective patterns, enabling structured exploration and refinement across mining iterations. During factor generation, QuantaAlpha enforces semantic consistency across the hypothesis, factor expression, and executable code, while constraining the complexity and redundancy of the generated factor to mitigate crowding. Extensive experiments on the China Securities Index 300 (CSI 300) demonstrate consistent gains over strong baseline models and prior agentic systems. When utilizing GPT-5.2, QuantaAlpha achieves an Information Coefficient (IC) of 0.1501, with an Annualized Rate of Return (ARR) of 27.75% and a Maximum Drawdown (MDD) of 7.98%. Moreover, factors mined on CSI 300 transfer effectively to CSI 500 and S&P 500, delivering 160% and 137% cumulative excess return over four years respectively.

## 1. Introduction

Financial markets are high-dimensional, non-stationary stochastic systems, where returns exhibit heavy tails, time-varying volatility, and cross-sectional dependence. Quantitative investment relies on alpha mining to extract predictive signals from noisy observations. LLMs and LLM-based agent frameworks have been introduced into the factor research workflow — automating factor construction and iteratively refining candidates through backtesting feedback.

Representative agent frameworks (AlphaAgent, RD-Agent) simulate human researcher workflow via hypothesis → factor construction → backtesting refinement. AlphaAgent imposes explicit regularization during factor generation to curb factor crowding and alpha decay. RD-Agent targets full-stack automation with coordinated research and development agents.

**Three limitations of existing systems under real-market non-stationarity and low SNR:**

1. **Fragile controllability** — iterative refinement driven by noisy backtest feedback induces semantic drift and steers updates toward spurious correlations.
2. **Limited trustworthiness** — many methods rely on stochastic re-generation conditioned on transient contexts, without explicitly inheriting validated rationales across iterations. Produced factors are hard to audit.
3. **Constrained exploration** — search over-exploits local neighborhoods around initial seeds, leading to redundancy and factor crowding, weakening long-horizon discovery.

**QuantaAlpha's three contributions:**

- **Diversified Planning Initialization** — generates multiple complementary research directions, broad initial coverage of hypothesis space.
- **Self-Evolution via trajectory-level Mutation + Crossover**.
  - Mutation: localize failure-causing step via self-reflection, rewrite only the corresponding segment.
  - Crossover: recombine complementary segments from high-reward parents.
- **Generation constraints** — semantic consistency + complexity/redundancy bounds to prevent drift and crowding.

## 2. Related Work

### Agents in Finance

Work on financial LLMs (FinGPT, Fin) + evaluation benchmarks (FinEval, FinMMR) → agentic systems (InvestorBench, FinVault) for decision-oriented research workflows including factor discovery and trading analysis. Alpha factor pursuit evolved from manual engineering and heuristic search toward LLM-driven closed-loop discovery. RD-Agent decouples the pipeline into research and development stages. AlphaForge focuses on dynamic combination of mined factors. AlphaFin and AlphaEval establish standardized, task-oriented protocols. **Trading constraints (turnover, complexity) remain largely post-hoc filters rather than intrinsic objectives**, leading to suboptimal generalization.

### Self-Evolving Agents

Self-evolving agents represent a paradigm shift from static instruction-following to autonomous learning through environment interaction. AlphaEvolve uses evolutionary resampling. CSE introduces controllable self-evolution. In finance: TradingAgents/FactorMAD use institutional debates; QuantAgents/ATLAS use simulated trading performance as reward; FinMem/FinCon use hierarchical memory. **Transition to finance is hindered by low SNR and non-stationarity.**

## 3. Problem Setup

### Alpha Mining Task

Learn alpha factor $f$ from market feature tensor $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times T \times D}$ for stock universe $\mathcal{S} = \{s_1, ..., s_N\}$ and time horizon $\mathcal{T} = \{t_1, ..., t_T\}$. At each $t$, factor produces signal from feature slice $\mathbf{X}_t$ to predict cross-sectional return $\mathbf{y}_{t+1}$.

$$ f^* = \arg\max_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \mathcal{L}(f(\mathbf{X}), \mathbf{y}) - \lambda \mathcal{R}(f) $$

where $\mathcal{F}$ = space of factor expressions, $\mathcal{L}$ = predictive effectiveness, $\mathcal{R}$ = simplicity/novelty regularizer.

### Alpha Mining Trajectory

Each run is an ordered sequence $\tau = (s_0, a_0, s_1, a_1, ..., s_n)$ where $s_0$ = initial mining context, $a_i$ = action at step $i$, $s_n$ = terminal state with evaluated result. Trajectory quality = terminal reward $R(\tau) = \mathcal{L}(f_\tau(\mathbf{X}), \mathbf{y}) - \lambda \mathcal{R}(f_\tau)$.

Objective: find trajectory-generation mechanism $\pi^* = \arg\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi}[R(\tau)]$.

## 4. Method

Four core components: (A) Diversified Planning Initialization; (B) Factor Realization with constraint gating; (C) Self-Evolution (mutation + crossover); (D) Final Factor Pool consolidation.

### 4.1 Alpha Mining Process

#### Hypothesis Generation

Idea agent $\mathcal{A}_i$ generates market hypotheses via structured knowledge integration. Conditioned on: (i) observations from current market conditions or previous mining trajectories, (ii) domain priors from financial theories, (iii) parametric specifications (e.g., "10-day high/low"). Produces actionable hypotheses describing candidate market mechanisms.

#### Controllable Factor Construction

Directly generating factor code is brittle → suffers from syntax errors, dependency mismatches, semantic drift. Solution: intermediate symbolic representation based on a standardized operator library $\mathcal{O}$ and an Abstract Syntax Tree (AST).

**Factor Generation.** Given hypothesis $h$ and raw features $\mathcal{X}$, the factor agent $\mathcal{A}_f$ generates symbolic expression $f \in \mathcal{F}$ over the operator library and parses it into an AST. Process:
1. Map $h$ to structured semantic description $d$ that formalizes the intended mechanism into discrete operators, while instantiating operational parameters (look-back windows, thresholds).
2. Assemble operator composition into symbolic expression $f$, parse into AST $T(f)$. Leaf nodes → raw feature fields (e.g., `$high`, `$volume`). Internal nodes → operator instances (`TS_MIN`, `SMA`, `RANK`).
3. Translate validated symbolic form into executable code $c$ via compilation. If compilation fails → LLM-based repair regenerates implementation while preserving semantics.

**Consistency Verification.** LLM-based verifier assesses (i) alignment among hypothesis $h$, semantic description $d$, symbolic expression $f$; (ii) faithfulness between symbolic definition $f$ and generated code $c$. If fails → rewrite inconsistent components.

**Complexity and Redundancy Control.**

Complexity:
$$ \mathcal{C}(f) = \alpha_1 \cdot SL(f) + \alpha_2 \cdot PC(f) + \alpha_3 \cdot \log(1 + |F_f|) $$
where $SL(f)$ = symbolic length, $PC(f)$ = free parameters count, $F_f$ = set of raw features used.

Redundancy: structural similarity via AST matching. For two factors $f_i, f_j$ with ASTs $T(f_i), T(f_j)$:
$$ s(f_i, f_j) = \max_{S_i \subseteq T(f_i), S_j \subseteq T(f_j), S_i \cong S_j} |V(S_i)| $$

Max similarity against existing alpha zoo $\mathcal{Z}$: $S(f) = \max_{\phi \in \mathcal{Z}} s(f, \phi)$. Reject and rewrite any factor violating complexity or redundancy limits.

#### Factor Evaluation

Evaluation agent $\mathcal{A}_e$ via standardized backtesting. Three aspects: predictive capability metrics, return performance metrics, risk control metrics. Maintains evaluation history — outcomes and systematic patterns among successful/failed factors.

### 4.2 Evolutionary Alpha Mining

Iterative optimization over mining trajectories. From seed factors → initial hypotheses → initial trajectory pool $\mathcal{T}_0$ → iterative evolution.

#### Diversified Planning Initialization

Initialization agent $\mathcal{A}_0$ proposes diversified initial hypotheses $\mathcal{H}^0 = \{h_1^0, ..., h_{N_{init}}^0\}$. Instructed to maximize complementarity at semantic AND structural levels — varying signal sources (price vs. volume), time scales (short vs. long), mechanism types (momentum vs. mean-reversion vs. regime-conditioned triggers).

#### Self-Evolution

Two primitives:

**Mutation.** Given trajectory $\tau$, self-reflection diagnoses the sub-optimal decision node (index $k$). Rewrite only the localized action:
$$ \tau_{child} = (s_0, a_0, ..., s_k, \text{Refine}(a_k), s'_{k+1}, a'_{k+1}, ..., s'_n) $$
Prefix up to $s_k$ frozen. Rewrite may update hypothesis, symbolic expression, or code. Can introduce mechanism-level changes (altering time scale, adding regime conditions).

**Crossover.** Select parent subset $\mathcal{P}_{i-1} \subseteq \mathcal{T}_{i-1}$ based on trajectory reward. Given $k$ parent trajectories, Crossover synthesizes a new child by composing high-performing segments:
$$ \tau_{child} = \text{Crossover}(\tau^{(1)}, ..., \tau^{(k)}) $$
Identifies segments that consistently contribute to high cumulative rewards — hypothesis templates, factor construction patterns, strategic repair actions.

## 5. Experiments

### Setup

- **Dataset**: CSI 300 (300 large-cap A-share stocks). Train: 2016-01-01 to 2020-12-31. Valid: 2021. Test: 2022-01-01 to 2025-12-26.
- **Metrics**: IC, ICIR, Rank IC, Rank ICIR (predictive power). ARR, IR, MDD, Calmar (strategy).
- **Baselines**: (1) ML (Linear, MLP, LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost, DoubleEnsemble); (2) deep learning (GRU, LSTM, Transformer, TRA); (3) classical factors (Alpha158, Alpha360); (4) LLM agents (RD-Agent, AlphaAgent).
- **Basic features**: `open`, `high`, `low`, `close`, `volume`, `vwap`. Label: $y_t = P^{close}_{t+2}/P^{close}_{t+1} - 1$. Preprocessing: forward-fill, replace inf, drop missing labels, CSRankNorm on features and labels.

### Main Results

Using GPT-5.2: QuantaAlpha achieves IC = 0.1501, ARR = 27.75%, MDD = 7.98%. Consistent gains across different backbone LLMs. Improves over RD-Agent by +0.0970 IC, +17.84% ARR, -6.84% MDD. Over AlphaAgent by +0.0535 IC, +12.21% ARR, -4.91% MDD.

### Ablation

- Removing **planning**: marginal change in IC/Rank IC, but ARR drops 7.78% and MDD increases 2.73%.
- Removing **mutation**: LARGEST drop in predictive power (IC -0.0292, Rank IC -0.0284), ARR -9.81%.
- Removing **crossover**: smaller but consistent degradation.
- Removing **consistency / complexity / redundancy** gates: disabling any single control degrades performance. Removing complexity is worst at strategy level (-8.44% ARR, +2.57% MDD).

### Factor Generalizability

Factors mined on CSI 300 transfer to CSI 500 and S&P 500 without re-optimization. Clear divergence emerges around December 2023 — baselines stagnate, QuantaAlpha sustains upward trajectory. By end of evaluation: ~160% cumulative excess return on CSI 500, >137% on S&P 500.

### Alpha Decay — 2023 A-share regime transition (critical)

**Market style transition 2023:** Pre-2023: large-cap core assets, high institutional participation, smooth intraday dynamics, persistent short-horizon trends — classical momentum/mean-reversion/exhaustion factors effective. In 2023: leadership rotates to small-cap and thematic stocks. Accompanied by increased intraday noise, frequent overnight gaps from call auctions, rapid cross-style liquidity re-allocation. Weakens trend persistence, amplifies path-dependent noise.

Baseline methods (Alpha158/360/AlphaAgent) fail to transfer to this out-of-distribution environment. QuantaAlpha discovers **more structural factors** that reflect persistent microstructure effects.

**Three channels that survive 2023 regime shift:**

1. **Overnight / auction information** — Gap-based factors aggregate information released during non-trading hours and price discovery in opening auction. Multiple QA factors occupy right tail of 2023 Rank IC distribution.
2. **Volatility structure and range-based signals** — Range deviation factors conditioned on volatility clustering capture abnormal variability rather than directional trends.
3. **Trend quality and liquidity re-rating** — QA emphasizes trend continuation ONLY when supported by low residual volatility and improving liquidity (rising dollar volume with limited price impact). Filters out noise-driven pseudo-trends.

### Evolutionary Efficiency

QuantaAlpha consistently maintains highest IC across all five iterations. Rapid gain in early rounds then stable at high level — strong sample efficiency. Moderate but persistent spread of IC across rounds — sustained diversity, not premature collapse.

### Case Study — Appendix B factor example

**Institutional_Momentum_Score_20D** (Round 10 Crossover, Direction 6):

$$ \text{IMS}_{20D} = \text{RANK}\left( \rho_{20}\left(\frac{\Delta P}{P}, \frac{\Delta V}{V}\right) \cdot \overline{\left(\frac{C - O}{C}\right)}_{5} \right) $$

Factor expression:
```
RANK(TS_CORR(DELTA(close, 1)/close, DELTA(volume, 1)/volume, 20) * TS_MEAN((close - open)/close, 5))
```

Captures institutional-driven momentum via (1) correlation between price returns and volume changes (coordinated institutional trading when positive); (2) average intraday return (close-open)/close pattern (institutional activity that influences closing prices).

Parent 1 (fragile momentum from retail speculation): Rank IC 0.0216.
Parent 2 (sustainable momentum from institutional activity): Rank IC 0.0246.
Offspring: Rank IC **0.0311**, IC 0.0126, ARR 7.80%, IR 0.963, MDD -11.37% (Baseline: 0.0220 / 0.0058 / 5.20% / 0.973 / -7.30%). Net: improved predictive power but higher drawdown (regime-adaptive weighting would fix this).

Synthesized hypothesis: regime-aware dual-source momentum combining institutional-driven structural momentum (validated by healthy microstructure) + retail-driven speculative momentum (high attention + deteriorating fundamentals), dynamically weighted by market volatility. Amplify institutional signals in stable regimes, retail reversal signals in turbulent regimes.

LLM Trajectory Summary Recommendations:
- Use 20-day price-volume correlation as institutional momentum proxy.
- Use 5-day average intraday returns as retail attention proxy.
- Add volatility regime indicator (recent / historical volatility ratio) for dynamic weighting.

### Iteration Convergence

Predictive performance does not scale linearly with iterations. Return and risk reach balanced high around iteration 11–12 (≈350 factors). Further iterations introduce redundant information, reduce robustness.

## 6. Conclusion

QuantaAlpha = self-evolving, interpretable alpha mining framework formulating factor discovery as constrained multi-agent research process. Three properties: diversity, controllability, trustworthiness. Future: multi-asset, adaptive regime-aware evolution, tighter integration with portfolio construction and risk management.

- Deal price = next-day opening
- Limit threshold 9.5%
